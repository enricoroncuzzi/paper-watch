# RAG retrieval and hybrid search

Retrieval-augmented generation answers from text retrieved at question time, so the quality ceiling is set by retrieval, not by the model. Vector search finds meaning ("tools for autonomous coding") but blurs exact names ("SWE-bench", "Qwen3"); keyword search finds exact names but misses paraphrase. Hybrid search runs both and fuses the two ranked lists with Reciprocal Rank Fusion: each item scores the sum of 1/(60 + rank) over the lists it appears in. RRF needs no score calibration between the two systems, which is why it is the standard first fusion method. Filters (tier, profile, date) are applied inside both queries so they shape the candidates, not just the final list. Only admitted items are searchable: candidates and irrelevant items live in the same table without an embedding, and the vector index covers admitted rows only.

## AND, OR and ranking

A full-text query is a boolean expression over word stems. `websearch_to_tsquery` joins every word with AND, which is precise for a name ("SWE-bench Verified") and useless for a paraphrase, where the paper uses other words for most of the question. Joining the stems with OR finds every item sharing any meaningful word, and cover-density ranking (`ts_rank_cd`) puts items matching more of the words, closer together, first. The filtering moves from the query to the ranking, and the fusion with the vector branch decides the rest. Stop words never become terms, so OR does not match "the" everywhere.

OR matching makes equal scores common in both branches: `ts_rank_cd` ties items whose text matches equally well, and the offline `FakeEmbedder` puts many items at the same cosine distance. Each branch therefore returns `(id, score)` rows and a shared `shared_ranks` helper gives equal scores the same rank (competition ranking, 1-2-2-4) before the fusion, so no item gains a rank from its random `uuid`. The stable `(source, external_id)` key then orders equal fused scores for output, and orders a branch list cut at `k`.

One limit is deliberate. On a corpus larger than `candidates`, a tie exactly at the candidate boundary decides which rows enter a branch, and the two branches differ. A vector tie at the boundary is vanishingly rare with real 384-dimension embeddings, though the eval's hashed fake embedder ties many rows. The text branch is the opposite: `ts_rank_cd` is heavily quantised, so a tie at the cut is routine (on a 100k-row corpus one paraphrase question matched every row with only dozens of distinct scores), and which tied rows enter is decided by the stable key. The eval's 41-item corpus is smaller than the 50 candidates, so every row with positive similarity is returned. A vector row at cosine distance 1 (similarity zero: the query and the item share no direction) is dropped before ranking, because it is not evidence; real English embeddings practically never reach it, but the fake embedder's orthogonal rows do.

OR matching ranks every row that shares a stem, so a natural question costs more than M1's AND query: measured on 100k synthetic rows, `text_query` took about 470 ms for a natural paraphrase question against 4 ms for AND (a single common stem cost the same under both). For M2's corpus that sits next to the chat model's latency and is acceptable. At the first corpus-size checkpoint (about 50k admitted rows), choose between a document-frequency stop list (`ts_stat`), keeping only the rarest stems, or a statement timeout.

## Length normalization

`ts_rank_cd` takes an optional third argument that divides its cover-density score by a function of the document length: 0 (the committed value) leaves the score as it is, 1 divides by 1 + log(length) and 2 divides by length. The corpus stores two very different document shapes: an arXiv item keeps only its abstract (98 to 308 words in the M1 live run), while a lab-blog item keeps the full post (16 of 50 live blog items are 400 to 6,358 words, the other 34 are short summaries). With OR matching, a long post almost always shares some stem with a question and collects more covers, so without normalization the text branch should prefer blog posts over papers. It does: on the M1 live database (216 admitted items: 158 vetted preprints, 8 peer-reviewed, 50 lab-blog posts), the text branch's top 5 for 12 fixed natural questions was 77% tier 3 against a fair share of 18% (the tier-3 share of every admitted item that matches the query at all). Normalization barely moves it, and the measured decision was not to adopt it:

| normalization | text-branch top-5 tier-3 share | fair share | median top-5 words | fake-embedder gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (none, committed) | 0.767 | 0.185 | 2139 | at the baseline |
| 1 | 0.700 | 0.185 | 1888 | at the baseline, but the live run loses one relevant item of 31 (hybrid recall@5 0.968 to 0.936, exact-name recall 1.000 to 0.944) |
| 2 | 0.683 | 0.185 | 25.5 | fails: `mrr` 0.863 to 0.847, `ndcg_at_k` 0.860 to 0.849, text-only exact-name MRR 0.889 to 0.824 |

Two things came out of that measurement. Normalization 2 swaps the long-post preference for a short-post preference and still leaves two thirds of the top 5 in tier 3, because blog items sit at both length extremes (34 of the 50 are far shorter than any abstract, 16 far longer): no single length function reaches the fair share. At normalization 0 the 46 blog slots (of 60) are filled by only 12 distinct posts, and one 4,380-word post takes 9 of them, which is a hub effect of a handful of giant documents. That the rest is topic vocabulary is a hypothesis, not a measurement: nothing here isolates it. Normalization 1 would de-quantise the text scores (the plateau at 1.4 becomes a spread of 0.29 to 0.67), but it costs a live exact-name item. On the fused ranking (vector branch plus text branch, the result `search` and the agent's `search_corpus` tool return) the real embedder brings the 12-question mean tier-3 share to 0.233 against the 0.185 fair share, not significant at this sample size (standard error 0.056); the five agentic-engineering questions, the primary topic, stay at 0.40 against 0.21. So `TS_RANK_NORMALIZATION` stays 0, named as a constant rather than a setting because it is a ranking decision, and a comment there points at this measurement; Task 14 logs the tier mix of its own tool results and re-measures on its queries before any tier correction is decided. A separate, parked limit: `ts_rank_cd` has no IDF, so common domain stems (`model`, `agent`, `learn`, `evalu`) weigh as much as rare discriminative ones; that, not document length, is what the ~50k-row checkpoint should fix, together with the stop list above.

The implementation is `src/paperwatch/retrieval/hybrid.py`, run by `paperwatch search QUERY --live` and covered by `tests/integration/test_hybrid.py`, which pins the fusion, the filters, the two rejection paths and the partial-index match.
